&emsp; Over the past year, I’ve gradually moved all my planning, learning, and project notes into **[Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/)**. If you haven’t heard of it, it’s a Markdown-based note-taking app that stores everything locally — so it’s fast, secure, and doesn’t lock you into a cloud platform.<!--more-->
What really sets it apart is how flexible it is. You can structure your notes however you want, use internal links between them, and extend the app with plugins. That last part is what really sold me.

---

## Drawing Ideas with Excalidraw

One plugin I use all the time is **Excalidraw**. It adds a freehand sketching canvas right inside Obsidian. Whenever I’m brainstorming or trying to map out a complex topic, I’ll open up an Excalidraw canvas and start drawing.

Sometimes it’s a quick flowchart. Other times it’s a visual outline of a project or lesson. The best part is that these drawings are just regular files in your vault — and you can link to them from anywhere.

## Making Obsidian Work Faster with Shortcuts

Another thing I’ve customized heavily: keyboard shortcuts. Obsidian lets you remap for a lof of actions

{% include aligner.html images="feature-img/obsidian_hotkeys.png" column=1 %}

A few useful:
- `Command pallet` opens command palette
- `Excalidraw: Create new drawing - IN A NEW TAB` opens Excalidraw new diagram.
- `Templates: Insert template` insert template from templates folder
- `Quick switcher: Open quick switcher` opens the quick switcher — super handy when jumping between notes
- `Create new note`
- `Add internal link`
- `Daily notes: Open today's daily note`

It doesn’t seem like much at first, but these shortcuts make a big difference when you’re jumping in and out of your notes all day.

## Staying Organized with the PARA Method

After trying a bunch of ways to organize my notes, the one that finally stuck was the **PARA method**. It was developed by Tiago Forte, and it’s surprisingly simple.

Here’s how it works:

- **Projects** — Active tasks or goals with a clear outcome (e.g. "Launch portfolio site")
- **Areas** — Ongoing responsibilities or roles (e.g. "Health", "Career", "Learning")
- **Resources** — Useful materials or reference notes (e.g. "Markdown tips", "Design patterns")
- **Archives** — Stuff you don’t need often, but want to keep (e.g. old projects, finished tasks)

Everything in your digital life fits into one of those four buckets. Once I set this up, it became way easier to find things — and to know where to put new notes.

On top of the above I am using obsidian **Daily** notes functionality (I am putting over there some notes that I am creating throughout the day, and later I might move them to any of the folders from PARA method above).

## A Real Example: Building a Course

To show how PARA works in practice, here’s how I structured my vault while working on a course about AI for developers:


````
📁 Projects
└── AI Course
    ├── outline.md
    ├── script-drafts.md
    └── visual-structure.excalidraw

📁 Areas
└── Learning
    ├── weekly-review.md
    └── obsidian-tips.md

📁 Resources
├── AI
│ ├── papers
│ │ └── transformer-architecture.md
│ └── tools
│   └── huggingface-cli.md
└── Teaching
    └── course-structure.md

📁 Archives
└── Old Projects
└── Python Bootcamp 2023
````

- The **AI Course** lives in **Projects** because I’m actively building it.
- Learning materials and regular reviews stay in **Areas**.
- Notes about tools and concepts go in **Resources**.
- Once the course is complete, it gets moved to **Archives**.
